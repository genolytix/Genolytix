Research overview

Making histopathology analysis more intelligent and interpretable

Our research explores how deep neural networks can learn discriminative patterns from tissue images, how model performance can be improved through iterative optimization, and how the geometry of learned representations can be studied.

A computational approach to digital pathology

Histopathological examination is central to cancer diagnosis. Digital pathology makes tissue images available for computational analysis. Our work investigates AI methods that can support the analysis of breast tissue histology images, with an initial focus on benign and malignant classification.

  • Image-based feature extraction and classification
  • Transfer learning with pretrained convolutional networks
  • Evolutionary optimization of model configurations
  • Geometric analysis of latent feature spaces

Research objective

To develop and evaluate a computational pipeline combining deep learning, iterative model improvement and differential-geometric analysis for histopathological image classification.

Primary application

Breast cancer histopathology using the BreakHis image dataset.

Core research areas

Three complementary layers of AI research

The project connects predictive modelling with automated model refinement and mathematical analysis of learned representations.

▦

Deep learning for histopathology

ResNet50 transfer learning is used to extract image features and build a classifier for breast histopathology images. The workflow includes image resizing, normalization, augmentation and dataset partitioning.

ResNet50 · BreakHis
↻

DGM-inspired self-improvement

An iterative evolution loop explores candidate model configurations by modifying hyperparameters and selected training settings. Candidates are trained, evaluated on validation data and compared to update the best-model archive.

Candidate generation · Selection
⌁

Manifold and Riemannian analysis

A convolutional autoencoder is used to study the learned feature manifold. Decoder Jacobians define a pullback metric, with spectral analysis and curvature estimates used to examine local representation geometry.

Autoencoder · Metric · Curvature
End-to-end methodology

A modular workflow designed to separate model development, model selection and final performance assessment.

Model improvement loop

Candidate configurations are generated and assessed using validation performance. The selected candidate becomes the starting point for the next iteration, while the test set remains reserved for final evaluation.

Representation geometry

ResNet50 feature representations are mapped through an autoencoder. The decoder-induced metric is analysed using Hessian-vector products and iterative eigensolvers such as Lanczos, alongside curvature estimation.

Experimental evaluation

Measuring classification performance

The evaluation framework considers overall performance as well as the ability to identify malignant cases.

AccuracyOverall correctness

SensitivityMalignant case detection

ROC-AUCDiscrimination ability

Reported experimental snapshot from a prior project discussion: on a 546-image test evaluation, the baseline-to-evolved comparison was reported as accuracy 91.03% → 96.89%, macro-F1 90.15% → 96.46%, malignant sensitivity 89.19% → 97.03%, and ROC-AUC 0.9754 → 0.9934. These figures should be checked against the final experiment logs, split protocol and statistical analysis before publication or external use.

Beyond the current study

Building towards robust and generalizable AI pathology

The next stage is to test the approach more broadly and establish the evidence needed for responsible translation into clinical research settings.

External validation

Evaluate performance on independent cohorts and images from different institutions, scanners and staining conditions.

Model robustness

Study sensitivity to image variation, augmentation, acquisition conditions and distribution shifts.

Explainability and geometry

Investigate links between latent-space structure, local geometric properties and classification behaviour.

Broader cancer applications

Explore adaptation of the computational framework to additional histopathology datasets and cancer types.

Clinical collaboration

Work with pathology experts to define relevant tasks, annotation protocols and clinically meaningful evaluation.

Responsible development

Prioritize reproducibility, patient-level data separation, transparent reporting and human oversight.

Research-use statement: This work describes an experimental AI research pipeline. It is not a clinically validated diagnostic system and is not intended to replace pathologist assessment or provide standalone medical diagnoses.
